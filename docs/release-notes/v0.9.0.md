Tenth pre-release of deckfold: video and sound that browsers do not play everywhere are converted, with the ffmpeg of your machine.

## Why

A video from an iPhone is HEVC, which Chrome and Firefox play only on some machines. A WMV or AVI video showed only its still image, and deckfold told you to convert it yourself. deckfold already converts pictures and fonts; this release does the same for video and sound.

## What changed

- **Video that not every browser plays becomes H.264 in an MP4 file.** That is HEVC, AV1, WMV, MPEG-2 and H.264 with 10 bits or 4:2:2. deckfold asks for a quality close to the source (`-crf 18`) and the slowest preset. Every frame keeps its time, also with a variable frame rate, and the colour tags of the source are set again.
- **H.264 in a MOV, MKV or AVI file is packed into an MP4 file.** The pictures are not encoded again; deckfold compares the packets before and after.
- **Sound: PCM in a WAV file becomes FLAC** when that is smaller. Both files decode to the same samples, and deckfold checks that. ADPCM in a WAV file and AIFF always become FLAC, because Chrome plays neither. WMA and the like become MP3, or AAC with more than two channels.
- **What every browser plays stays as it is,** byte for byte: H.264 with AAC or MP3 in MP4, MP3, AAC, FLAC, Opus, and WAV files with floating point, mu-law or A-law.
- **This needs `ffmpeg` and `ffprobe` on the PATH.** deckfold brings no encoder for video or sound. Without ffmpeg, or when the installed one lacks an encoder, the file is embedded as it is and the report says so.
- **The report names the converted videos and sounds** and what stays: a video with HDR, with transparency or interlaced is not converted.
- **Ctrl+C stops the conversion,** also in the middle of a video, and removes the temporary folders. Nothing is written then.
- **`--keep-assets` switches this off too.** `DECKFOLD_NO_FFMPEG=1` ignores an installed ffmpeg.

## Why not an encoder inside deckfold

That was the plan: an ffmpeg built as WebAssembly inside the binary, so that nothing has to be installed. It was measured. With one core and without assembler it needs 7 seconds for one frame of a 1080p video and 18 seconds for one frame of 4096 x 2160, about 19 hours for the three HEVC videos of a local collection. The installed ffmpeg converts them in under ten minutes. With x264 inside, deckfold would also have had to change its licence to the GPL. It stays under the MIT licence. The numbers are in `docs/spikes/S9-video-and-sound.md`.

## What this does not cover

- The result for video and sound depends on the ffmpeg of the machine. Two machines can write different bytes for the same presentation. Pictures and fonts are as before the same everywhere.
- Converted video and sound are not kept in `.deckfold-cache`. deckfold cannot check a kept video against its source the way it checks a picture, so a video is converted again at every conversion.
- Video with HDR, with transparency or interlaced stays as it is, with a warning.
- ffmpeg reads the files of the presentation outside any sandbox. deckfold passes it only files whose container it has told from the first bytes itself, always names the format, limits the protocols to files and stops a run that makes no progress for five minutes. A playlist or a script that names other files never reaches ffmpeg as such.
- Whether shipping or using an H.264 or AAC encoder needs a patent licence where you are is your matter and that of your ffmpeg; deckfold ships none.

## What is tested

- The rules, in unit tests that make their clips with ffmpeg: every container deckfold knows; WAV to FLAC with the same samples; ADPCM, AIFF, WMA, six channels; H.264 in Matroska packed again with the same number of packets; HEVC with 10 bits, WMV with and without sound, MPEG-2, H.264 with 10 bits and 4:2:2, and MPEG-4 in AVI to H.264 High, 8 bits 4:2:0, with every frame and the colour tags; HDR, transparency and interlacing left alone with a note.
- Data that only looks like a video: headers followed by noise, by a playlist or by a concat script that name another file. Nothing is converted, nothing hangs, and the other file is not in the result.
- Without ffmpeg: every file stays, and the report says so only for files ffmpeg would convert.
- A run that reports no progress is stopped; a cancelled conversion leaves the file as it is.
- A PowerPoint deck with a WMV video and a WAV sound: the slide plays an MP4 and a FLAC file. Without ffmpeg the deck is what it was in v0.8.0.
- An HTML page with a video and a sound: both are converted, the `type` of the `<source>` follows, and no cache folder is made.
- In the browser (`av.spec.mjs`), in Chrome and WebKit on a Mac: the converted video shows its picture at the right size and plays, the FLAC file loads at its exact length.
- Nine decks of a local collection with ffmpeg 9.0.2: three HEVC videos were converted, two of 4096 x 2160 with 10 bits (65.2 MB became 58.5 MB) and one of 1920 x 1080 (324 MB became 178 MB), in 238 and 337 seconds for their decks. The H.264 videos stayed. The three converted videos play in Chrome and in WebKit (Playwright) at their size and length.
- In Chrome and WebKit on a Mac: the 148 browser tests. The decks of the setup that have a WAV sound now play it as FLAC.
- The Go suite with and without ffmpeg (`DECKFOLD_NO_FFMPEG=1`).
- The six binaries build with `scripts/release.sh` on a Mac (19.3 MB to 21.2 MB), and the smoke test passes with the one for macOS (arm64).
- The CI: `go vet`, the Go suite and the smoke test on Linux, Windows and macOS (x64 and arm64), and the browser tests with the public decks in Chrome on Linux and Windows and in Firefox on Linux. The Linux runners have ffmpeg installed for this, so the tests of video and sound run on Linux (x64) and the converted video and sound play in Chrome and in Firefox there; on the other runners these tests are skipped.

## Not tested yet

- Windows with ffmpeg installed. The code that finds and calls ffmpeg is the same, but no test ran it there.
- Other versions of ffmpeg than 9.0.2 (Homebrew) and the one of the Linux runner.
- Video with HDR, transparency or interlacing from a real source; the tests make such clips with ffmpeg.
- Stopping with Ctrl+C in the middle of a video, by hand. The tests cover a cancelled conversion, not the key.
- An HTML export with pictures to convert from a real source, Safari itself, and the rows of `docs/manual-test.md`, among them the new one for converted video and sound.
