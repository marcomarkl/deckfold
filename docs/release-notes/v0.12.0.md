Fourteenth pre-release of deckfold: slides can be hidden from the show, and a slide hidden in PowerPoint is now in the file.

## What changed

- Every tile on the start screen has an eye at the right of its number. A click hides the slide from the show; the tile is greyed out and the eye crossed. Another click shows it again. The choice is stored in the browser for each presentation (decision E21).
- The show goes past a hidden slide on next, back, Home, End, the links to the next and previous slide, and in auto mode, also when auto mode loops back to the start. The number of a hidden slide with Enter, a hit of the search or a link to that slide still shows it, and the show goes on with the next slide that is not hidden.
- The show always begins on a slide that is not hidden. When the chosen start slide is hidden, the line beside "Start" says where the show begins, for example "Starts at slide 5". The last slide that is not hidden cannot be hidden.
- While slides are hidden, the count on the start screen and in the status bar of the presenter view reads "7 of 9 slides".
- The strip of slides in the presenter view leaves hidden slides out and follows a click on an eye at once, not with the next sign of life a second later. The tiles keep their numbers.
- In a PowerPoint deck the next slide after a hidden one comes in with its own transition, as in PowerPoint. claude.ai and Keynote decks jump past the hidden slide.
- A slide hidden in PowerPoint ("Hide Slide") is now in the file, hidden from the show until its eye is clicked. Until v0.11.0 deckfold left such slides out. Their text, pictures and notes are therefore in the file now and can be seen on the start screen and with `--content`. To pass on a file without them, convert with `--drop-hidden`, or set `"dropHidden": true` in the configuration file; such slides are then left out as in v0.11.0, and a link to one of them stays text. A file converted before needs a new conversion for this; `--refresh` does not add slides.
- A link to a slide hidden in PowerPoint now leads there (decision P18).
- There is no eye for a self-playing Keynote export or a file of an unknown format, where deckfold does not move the player itself.

## What is tested

- Browser tests for each change: the eye on every tile, its place at the right of the number, its state and name; the stored choice after a reload and the deck's own choice when nothing is stored; the count; the hint beside "Start"; the strip of the presenter view within 300 ms of a click, three times in a row; next, back, End, the preview and a jump by number; a hidden start slide and a hidden first slide; auto mode with loop; the links over a hidden slide and to it. The 11 new and 11 changed tests fail on the code of v0.11.0; of the changed image comparison with PowerPoint, the two decks with a hidden slide fail there, and the other decks are checked as before.
- For PowerPoint, claude.ai and Keynote decks, that no frame shows the hidden slide when the show goes past it, and for PowerPoint, that the next slide comes in with its transition. With the comparison of v0.11.0 in the code that moves the player, the PowerPoint and claude.ai players showed the hidden slide for 1.1 and 1.4 seconds, and the Keynote player reported a change to it; with a jump instead of the new call of the PowerPoint player, the transition was missing. The claude.ai and Keynote checks ran with local decks only; the CI has none.
- Go tests: a slide hidden in PowerPoint is drawn, marked and in the content; a link to it leads there; `--drop-hidden` and `dropHidden` give the output of v0.11.0, with the warning for such a link; the switch is not written into the file.
- The start screen with hidden slides and the presenter view before and during the show, at 1280 x 800.
- In Chrome and WebKit on a Mac: the 175 browser tests (150 in Chrome, 25 in WebKit).
- The six binaries build with `scripts/release.sh` on a Mac (19.3 MB to 21.2 MB), and the smoke test passes with the one for macOS (arm64).
- The CI: `go vet`, the Go suite and the smoke test on Linux, Windows and macOS (x64 and arm64), and the browser tests with the public decks in Chrome on Linux and Windows and in Firefox on Linux.
