Twelfth pre-release of deckfold: the presenter view is laid out anew. What the audience sees stands large on the left, the right pane shows what the next click brings, and a row of dots tells how many clicks are left on the slide.

## What changed

- **The presenter view shows what is on screen and what the next click brings.** Until now its large pane showed the current slide after all its steps, a smaller pane below it what the audience saw, and "Next" the next slide after all its steps, although the next click often showed only a step of the current slide. Now there are three panes, arranged like PowerPoint's presenter view:
  - "On screen", large on the left, shows what the audience sees: the slide at its current step, a black or white screen, the end. Before the start it shows the slide the show will start on, instead of an empty pane.
  - "Next click", at the top right, shows the state the next click leads to: the next step of the slide, or the next slide at its first step. When that click changes the slide, the pane is marked and its heading reads "Next slide".
  - The notes stand below it.

  The pane that showed the current slide after all its steps is gone. Decision E20; it revises E5 and, for the presenter view, the previews of E14.
- **A dot for each click.** The heading of "On screen" has a dot for each step of the slide, filled up to the one the audience sees, and an arrow for the change to the next slide. Every mark after the last filled dot is one click, so the clicks left on a slide can be seen without reading a number. When the next click changes the slide, the arrow is marked as well. A slide with 13 steps or more shows a bar instead of the dots. Steps that run by themselves on claude.ai slides take no dot, and the step counter in the top bar now counts the same steps.
- **Time and the slide strip are always shown.** Their switches are gone; the status bar keeps three, for "On screen", "Next click" and the notes.
- **Settings of earlier versions are still read.** A file refreshed with `--refresh`, a configuration file, `--presenter-elements` and the settings stored in the browser may name the panes of earlier versions. `current` becomes `audience` ("On screen"), and `time` and `overview` are dropped. A list that was the default of an earlier version becomes today's default. The share of the removed pane in the layout is ignored. `--presenter-elements` now takes `audience,next,notes`.

## Firefox and animated GIF pictures

A GIF starts again from its first frame when its slide is entered again. In Firefox that first frame can stay on screen for a shorter time than the file says. Firefox keeps all animated pictures of a window on one common time grid, and a picture that starts again shows its first frame only until the next point of that grid: between 0 and 500 ms on the test deck, where the file says 500 ms. Chrome shows it for the full time. deckfold cannot move that grid. The browser test of the restart failed now and then in Firefox for this reason (3 of 100 repeats); it now enters the slide again at a known point of the grid.

## What is tested

- The panes of the presenter view in the browser tests: "On screen" before the start, during the show, on black, white and the end slide; "Next click" moving with every step and showing the next slide at its first step after the last one; the dots and the arrow on a slide with four steps and on the next slide; the three switches; and a list of v0.9.1 stored in the browser. The four new tests fail on the code of v0.9.1.
- Go tests for the settings: the default lists of earlier versions, `current`, `time` and `overview` in a configuration and from `--presenter-elements`, an unknown name, and the share of the removed pane.
- A file made by v0.9.1 and refreshed with this version has the panes `audience`, `next` and `notes`, and no share of the removed pane.
- The presenter view at 1280 x 800 before the start, in the middle and at the last step of a slide with steps, at the end, with notes, and at 700 px wide.
- The GIF restart test passed 200 repeats in Firefox on the Linux runner, where 3 of 100 had failed before; the measurements of the time grid are in pull request #26.
- In Chrome and WebKit on a Mac: the 155 browser tests.
- The six binaries build with `scripts/release.sh` on a Mac (19.3 MB to 21.2 MB), and the smoke test passes with the one for macOS (arm64).
- The CI: `go vet`, the Go suite and the smoke test on Linux, Windows and macOS (x64 and arm64), and the browser tests with the public decks in Chrome on Linux and Windows and in Firefox on Linux.
