Eleventh pre-release of deckfold: the presentation keeps the screen saver away in Safari too and tells the presenter when it cannot, two faults in PowerPoint decks are fixed (photos in EMF pictures and the text colour of shapes), and the start slide no longer gets lost in a reload.

## What changed

- **The screen saver stays away in Safari too, and the presenter view says when it does not.** The presentation window asks the browser for a screen wake lock. Safari grants one only shortly after a click or key, and not right after full screen was requested in the same click; deckfold asked after the start had finished, so Safari never held the lock. The click on "Start" now asks for it before full screen. While no lock is held, the presentation asks again on every key, click and command, when it becomes visible again, and every 10 seconds after a refusal. When the browser does not hold the lock, for example while the presentation window is minimized, the status line of the presenter view says "Screen saver not blocked". The audience sees nothing of it. Decision E19; the measurements are in `docs/spikes/S10-wake-lock.md`.
- **Photos inside an EMF picture show whole.** A bitmap in an EMF picture was drawn with its clip on the picture element itself. A browser reads such a clip in the coordinates of the picture, so it was scaled with the picture and left only a strip of each photo. On a slide with three photos of sun blinds in one EMF picture, only their edges showed. This was so since PowerPoint files are read directly, not only since v0.7.0.
- **Text keeps the colour of its shape style.** A shape inserted in PowerPoint carries a style, and that style gives its text a colour, usually white. deckfold let the text colour of the master win over it, so white text in coloured shapes came out black. PowerPoint ranks the colour of the style above what a shape inherits from the presentation, the master and the layout, and below the shape's own text styles and the colour of the text itself. deckfold now does the same.
- **The start screen keeps the slide chosen or shown last across a reload.** deckfold kept it in the session storage of the browser and reloaded right after writing it, at the end of a show for example. Chrome sends such a write on a channel of its own, and the reload can overtake it: on a Windows test machine the start screen lost the slide in 4 of 150 repeats. The slide now also travels with the reload itself, in the address on the start screen and at the end of a show, and in the state of the browser history during a show.

## Looked into, and not changed

A presentation in Safari was reported with an empty presenter view, photos that looked more pixelated than before, and a slow feel. These were measured in Safari 27.2 on a Mac with 36 GB, with a deck of 108 slides converted by v0.6.0, by v0.8.0, by this version, and by this version with `--keep-assets`:

- The photos on a slide are the same in all four, pixel for pixel, in a screenshot of 5120 x 2880.
- The two videos that v0.9.0 converts from HEVC to H.264 measure SSIM 0.996 and PSNR 52 to 53 dB against their source.
- The start screen came after 9.3 s with v0.6.0 and 5.6 s with this version. A move to another slide took 5 to 77 ms in all four; with the presenter view open, up to 0.5 s on slides with large photos, in v0.6.0 as well.
- The presenter view showed its counter, notes and three previews in each run. Safari used 5.9 GB with v0.6.0 and 4.2 GB with this version while both windows were open.

None of the three was reproduced, so nothing was changed for them. If the presenter view stays empty again, a screenshot of it and the console of that window (Develop > Web Inspector) will show what happened.

## What the wake lock does not cover

- No web page can stop a screen lock that a company policy or a device management profile enforces.
- Whether the lock keeps the screen saver away was measured in Chrome on a Mac only. Safari grants the lock, and macOS lists it, but an idle run with Safari was not made. Windows, Linux and Firefox are not measured.
- After Safari has let go of the lock, it gives it back only on a key or click in the presentation window. Keys pressed in the presenter view do not count for that.

## What is tested

- A unit test builds an EMF picture with a clipped bitmap and checks that no element carries both its own transform and the clip. It fails on the code of v0.9.0.
- On the deck the fault was found on, the first EMF picture had 31 188 non-white pixels before and 396 611 after, drawn at 1082 x 506, in Chrome, WebKit and Safari. Of nine local decks only this one has bitmaps under a clip, and only its two EMF pictures changed.
- The text2 feature deck has a new slide with five shapes: the style colour alone, with a text style of the shape, with a run colour, without a style, and a title placeholder with a style. PowerPoint's image of the slide gives the colours, and a test compares deckfold's with them; it fails on the code of v0.9.0. The slide measures SSIM 0.993 against PowerPoint.
- On nine local decks exactly six texts changed colour, all in shapes with a style colour.
- The GIF timing test takes at least 60 screenshots now; a slow runner once took only 10 and the test failed for that reason.
- The test of the start slide across a reload passed 150 repeats in Chrome on the Windows runner, where 4 of 150 had failed before. It also checks that the address is empty once a player runs, and that ending a show right after a move chooses that slide.
- The wake lock, measured by hand on a Mac with macOS 27.2.1 and `pmset -g assertions`: in Chrome 158 the lock held off the screen saver through an idle time of 699 seconds, with the screen saver set to 600; after the browser closed, the screen saver started by itself. In Safari 27.2 the lock was granted with the new code and refused with the old one. In Chrome a presentation window covered by the presenter view kept the lock; a minimized one lost it, the presenter view showed the hint, and the lock came back with the window.
- Six browser tests of the wake lock use a stand-in for the browser's lock: no request before "Start", the first one in the click and before full screen, a new one after a release, after a refusal and on a key, no second request while one is open, and the hint in the presenter view for a refused, an open and a missing lock. All six fail on the code of v0.9.0.
- The presenter view with the hint at 1280 x 800, in German and English.
- Three browser tests that failed now and then on the Windows runner measure what they are meant to: the 200-slide performance test times a step from the key to the player changing slide, read in the presentation window; the fade test starts the fade once the page draws frames steadily, which passed 100 repeats on the Windows runner; and the test of a player brought back to deckfold's state moves and reads the player in one call.
- In Chrome and WebKit on a Mac: the 154 browser tests.
- The six binaries build with `scripts/release.sh` on a Mac (19.3 MB to 21.2 MB), and the smoke test passes with the one for macOS (arm64).
- The CI: `go vet`, the Go suite and the smoke test on Linux, Windows and macOS (x64 and arm64), and the browser tests with the public decks in Chrome on Linux and Windows and in Firefox on Linux.
