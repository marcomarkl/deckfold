Seventh pre-release of deckfold: PowerPoint text stands where PowerPoint puts it, and the start screen fits its window.

## Why

deckfold lays out the text of a PowerPoint deck itself. Until now it did not kern, and it placed every baseline by the ascent of the font alone. Lines were up to 0.7 % longer than PowerPoint's, single words up to 11 %, and broke in other places. Baselines were 1 to 3 px off, and several pixels at a line spacing of 150 %.

The start screen listed every key below the settings and ran 130 px out of a window of 1280 x 800.

## What changed

**PowerPoint decks**

- **Kerning as PowerPoint does it.** A run is kerned when its font size, as drawn, is at least the value of its `kern` attribute, with the pairs of the font's `kern` table, also across runs and with the space. PowerPoint does not read the newer GPOS table, so neither does deckfold. The rule is the same for all fonts: it was measured on 17 of them against the PDF export of PowerPoint 16.114.4 for Mac, and nothing is tuned per font.
- **Bundled fonts kern like the font they stand in for.** Where the font of the deck is not installed, deckfold draws Carlito for Calibri and Liberation for Arial, Helvetica and Times New Roman. They kern only where they have the pairs of the original. Carlito Bold kerns 51 pairs more than Calibri Bold and is used all the same; oblique Helvetica is not kerned.
- **Baselines where PowerPoint places them.** A single line is 1.2 times the font size, divided by the ascender and the descender of the font; the line spacing is the distance from baseline to baseline. One formula fits 132 measured baselines in 44 combinations of line spacing, anchor and font.
- **A slide number counts hidden slides,** as PowerPoint does. A deck whose first slide is hidden showed 8 on the slide PowerPoint numbers 9.
- **Pictures in files without a picture extension are drawn.** PowerPoint for Mac stores some SVG and EMF pictures as `.bin` files and names their format only in the content types of the file; such pictures were left out without a word. A picture in a format deckfold does not know is now named in the report.
- **A file converted before keeps its old slides.** `--refresh` updates the controls and does not draw slides again. Convert the presentation again to get the new text layout, the slide numbers and the pictures.

**Keynote exports and the presenter view**

- **Previews of a Keynote slide show every build.** The Keynote player draws a build itself and loses a key that comes while it is still drawing. deckfold pressed the keys of a jump 250 ms apart, and with builds of half a second the previews ended one build short: three of four list entries on the start screen and in the "Current" pane. The jump now waits as long as each build takes, which the export says. A preview of a slide with builds shows them one after the other before it rests. Convert the export again for this; a file from before waits for builds of half a second.
- **A second deck in the same tab leaves the presenter view of the first alone.** It took that window over: its switch was on without a click, while the window still showed the first deck. It also started at the slide chosen in the first deck. The session of a deck is now its own.

**Start screen**

- **It fits the window.** The settings stand in one line, the title and "Start" are smaller, and the preview takes the height the other rows leave, up to 42 % of the window. Nothing has to be scrolled at 1280 x 800.
- **The keys are behind a button.** A button with a keyboard icon opens the list of every key as it is set, with the version of deckfold below it. Escape, the × or a click beside the list closes it. While it is open no key reaches the start screen.
- **A last row below the settings:** the button for the keys, "Search" and the number of slides. The title stands in the middle.
- **The search has a close button** too, on the start screen and in the presenter view. Tab moves to it, Enter and Space press it.
- "Forward" stands once in the list of keys, with the click after its keys.

## What this does not cover

- The kerning and the baselines are measured on the PDF export of PowerPoint for Mac, which is also where the reference images come from. PowerPoint for Windows and the slide show are not measured, and neither are fonts with CFF outlines.
- Whether the whole points the baselines sit on are PowerPoint's layout or its PDF export is not known.
- Two cases do not fit the baseline formula by one point: Century Gothic Regular at 28 pt and the embedded font of one test deck.
- The list of keys opens with the mouse only. It has no key of its own, and Enter always starts on the start screen.

## What is tested

- Kerning: where deckfold puts every word of the public kerning deck against where PowerPoint put it, in the converter and in the browser (Chrome and Firefox). The kerning of the bundled Carlito against the values recorded when it was compared with Calibri.
- Baselines: 132 baselines of a deck made for it against the ones read from PowerPoint's PDF.
- Image comparison with PowerPoint for every feature deck. The kerning deck passes at a threshold of its own, 0.92 instead of 0.95: its slides measure 0.935 to 0.985, lower on the Linux and Windows runners than on the Mac, and a slide without kerning loses as much there.
- Slide numbers with a hidden slide and with a first slide number; SVG, WMF and PNG pictures stored as `.bin` files, and the report for a file that is no picture.
- Ten decks of a local collection, 336 slides, against the pages of PowerPoint's PDF, drawn with v0.5.0 and with this version: 269 slides are closer to PowerPoint, 64 unchanged and 3 farther, by 0.013 at most. The mean SSIM rose from 0.937 to 0.959. One deck holds a video of 1.1 GB, which deckfold does not embed; its slides were measured on a copy without that video.
- The start screen in Chrome, WebKit and Firefox: the footer, the list of keys and its keys, the close buttons, and the layout at three window sizes, with a title of two lines and with slides in 4:3, portrait and banner shape.
- The start screen without container units, where the preview keeps a fixed share of the window height. All three test browsers have the units, and Chrome has no switch for them, so the test takes that block out of the loaded style sheet; what else an older browser lacks it does not show.
- A jump to the last step of a Keynote slide with builds against the picture that single steps give, on a local export; a second deck in the same tab.
- In a visible Chrome with real windows, on a Mac with two screens, by automation: the 119 browser tests, and the rows of `docs/manual-test.md` that need no more than a real window. The presenter view opens without a prompt and keeps its place and size at "Start" and at the end, on both screens; audio plays after the click on "Start" without the autoplay permission the tests set; a setting of the presenter view survives a restart of the browser; auto mode goes on for 150 s while the presenter view is minimized.
- The six binaries build with `scripts/release.sh`.

## Not tested yet

- What automation cannot do, from `docs/manual-test.md`: real full screen (the presentation, the presenter view, Escape, a link in full screen), dragging the presenter view between the screens, the highlight under a resting mouse on the second screen, a clicker, a swipe on the trackpad, one screen only, and Safari. These rows were not run for this pre-release.
- Keynote builds of another duration than half a second.
- One test, for links to other slides in the presenter view, failed once in Firefox on the CI runner and passed in the runs after it. Forty repeats in Chrome all passed. The cause is not known.
